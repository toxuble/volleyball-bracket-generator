Timing method

Every match is one rally-scoring set to the displayed point target, win by two, with no point cap. Matches end by score; the clock never ends a match. All warm-ups, court changes, and rest breaks are excluded from the event estimate.

How point targets are chosen

The planner uses targets of up to 15 for early rounds, 17 for quarterfinals, 19 for semifinals, and 21 for the championship. Each later round has a higher target. Short windows scale targets down to a minimum of five points. The adjustable overrun buffer defaults to 10% of the total event window and is excluded from the planned play budget. Any additional unallocated time is shown separately: it is not disguised as longer play.

Playing-time estimates interpolate between the USAV benchmarks and extrapolate for shorter targets, rounding up to whole minutes. These are planning estimates, not measured predictions for your teams.

Courts, byes, and uncertainty

Matches are grouped into simultaneous court waves, and every round finishes before the next starts. Byes consume no time. Team size changes the player count, not the timing estimate: player skill and rally length matter more than roster size. No guaranteed end time is possible with uncapped win-by-two scoring. Leave extra time outside the play window for warm-ups and breaks if needed.

The USA Volleyball reference also lists 65 minutes for best-of-three and 105 minutes for best-of-five, including warm-up and between-set allowances. This planner uses one-set matches so each round has a clear point target.
