How point targets are chosen

The planner starts with relative targets of 15 for early rounds, 21 for quarterfinals, 25 for semifinals, and 35 for the championship, where those rounds exist. It scales these targets to use the available time, then rounds to whole points. Each later round has a higher target and a longer estimated duration. Targets are capped at those reference targets rather than inflated into extremely long sets. Earlier rounds with the same reference have successively lower caps to keep every later round longer. Short windows scale targets down to a minimum of five points; longer windows leave reserve time.

Playing-time estimates interpolate between the 15-point and 25-point benchmarks and extrapolate for other targets. They round up to whole minutes. This is an organizer planning model, not a measured prediction for your teams. Custom targets such as 21 or 35 are recreational formats, not official indoor FIVB scoring.

Courts, byes, and uncertainty

Matches are grouped into simultaneous court waves, and every round finishes before the next starts. Byes consume no time. Team size changes the player count, not the timing estimate: player skill and rally length matter more than roster size. No guaranteed end time is possible with uncapped win-by-two scoring. Leave extra time outside the play window for warm-ups and breaks if needed.

The USA Volleyball reference also lists 65 minutes for best-of-three and 105 minutes for best-of-five, including warm-up and between-set allowances. This planner uses one-set matches so each round has a clear point target.
